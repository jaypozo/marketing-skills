# Qualifying a partner

Screen first (active in the last 60 days, plausibly reaches the buyer), then score only the survivors. Base scores on their recent content rather than their bio.

## Scorecard

Audience fit (0 to 3)

- 3: their audience is mostly the user's buyers, at a moment when they'd need the product.
- 2: a large share overlaps, or the timing fits well.
- 1: some overlap, and you'd need to stretch to explain why it fits.
- 0: you can't point to evidence of overlap.

Engagement (0 to 3)

- 3: comments, replies and questions from their audience on recent posts or issues, written by people, not bots.
- 2: steady but modest response.
- 1: large numbers with little response.
- 0: signs of bought followers.

If you can't see engagement (a private newsletter, say), score 1 and mark it "unverified". A public archive, or whether they answer replies, is a fair clue.

Standards (0 to 3)

- 3: careful, accurate content the user would be proud to appear beside.
- 2: fine, with occasional weak pieces.
- 1: mostly sponsored or thin content.
- 0: spam, misleading claims or anything that would embarrass the user.

Active: posted or published in the last 60 days? This is a pass or fail gate, not part of the score. If they fail but fit unusually well, put them on the "revisit later" list.

Keep prospects scoring 7 or more out of 9 (fit, engagement and standards). Keep a 6 only if the contribution fits them unusually well.

## Signs to look for

- They already work with partners: sponsor pages, "work with us", guest bylines, affiliate disclosures, co-hosted events.
- Their audience asks questions the user can answer.
- They've mentioned the problem the user solves.
- They link out to useful resources, so they're used to recommending others.

## Red flags

- Link-selling pages ("guest post: $150, do-follow").
- Audience numbers they won't share, or that don't match their visible engagement.
- Recent content that conflicts with the user's values or claims.
- A direct competitor in disguise.
- Existing deals with the user's competitors, or with categories on the user's avoid list.

## Where to find the contact

Use public routes only: the contact page, the author bio, the newsletter reply address, the "work with us" form, or a professional profile. Don't guess personal emails or scrape addresses.

## Partner types by customer moment

Prompts for step 1 of the procedure. Customers of consumer products often follow creators and communities more than businesses, so include both.

- Before buying: who does the customer ask for advice? What do they read, watch or join while researching?
- During: what else do they buy or set up at the same time? Who installs, delivers or trains?
- After: what's the next problem they have? Who solves it?
