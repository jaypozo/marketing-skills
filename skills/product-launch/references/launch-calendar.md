# Launch calendar and retrospective

Day 0 is launch day. Move the whole calendar if the date moves.

## Four to two weeks before (day -28 to day -14)

- Confirm the offer, price and any launch offer end date in writing.
- Talk about the problem the product solves in your normal channels. No hard selling yet.
- Run the expectation math now. If it falls short, partners need these weeks of notice.
- If the product isn't public and there's no list, open a waitlist page and count signups as a demand check. If there is a list, ask readers one question instead ("What would you want from this?") and count the replies.
- Ask beta users for a short quote, and get permission to publish it with their name or initials.
- Line up partners: what they'll send, when, their tracking code, and the copy they need from you.
- Draft every email and post on this calendar. Final versions are due two days before each one goes out.

## The week before (day -7 to day -1)

- Show a preview: a short demo clip, screenshots, the launch date.
- Email the list: what's coming and when, plus what early-access people get, if there's an early-access group.
- Finish the product page, checkout and delivery. Do a real test purchase and refund.
- Send partners their kit (copy, images, link, code) at least five days ahead.
- Write the FAQ and ready-made replies. Set up the support inbox and notifications.
- Write the launch-day run sheet.

## Follow-up week (day 1 to day 7)

- Day 2: answer the most common question publicly, in a post or short email.
- Day 3 or 4: share a real early result or reaction, with permission.
- Last day of the offer, or the day before: if the launch offer has an end date, send one reminder saying exactly when. Leave out people who already bought.
- Close the launch offer on time. Update the page.
