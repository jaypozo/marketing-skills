# Scoring keyword groups

Score each group on four things, then sort. The scores make you write a reason for every priority. Small differences between scores mean little.

## Business value (0 to 3)

- 3: the searcher is ready to buy something the business sells ("buy", "price", "best [product] for", a product type).
- 2: the searcher is comparing options or solving a problem the product clearly helps with, and there's a natural next step to a product.
- 1: education on the topic, with a weak or distant path to the product.
- 0: no plausible path to the product, or the wrong audience.

## Ability to win (0 to 3)

Open the top 10 results.

- 3: weak results: forums, thin posts, outdated pages, or pages that answer a different question.
- 2: decent results, but the business has something they lack: its own data, real photos, hands-on experience, a tool, a better format.
- 1: strong, specialist or very large sites, and the business has little to add.
- 0: dominated by brands, government or medical sources, or a format the business can't produce (a map pack for a business with no local presence, for example).

When a group mixes a winnable query with an unwinnable one, score the main query.

## Demand

1. Use monthly figures. Divide Search Console totals by the number of months in the export.
2. Where the site already ranks in the top 20 for the group, use Search Console impressions. They're real counts. Otherwise use the keyword tool's estimate. Write the source beside the number.
3. Sort the groups by demand, then split them into thirds: High, Medium and Low. With fewer than 20 groups, finer tiers mean nothing.
4. Groups with no volume data at all go in Low, marked "no data".

Tool estimates for small queries are often off by a factor of two or more, so tiers tell you more than raw numbers.

## Click opportunity

- Full: organic results start near the top of the page.
- Reduced: ads, an AI answer or a map pack sit above the first organic result. A shopping box counts as reduced for an article, but as an opportunity for a shop with a product feed.
- Poor: the answer appears on the results page itself (a definition, a conversion, a time), so few people click.

## Sort order

Apply these steps in order:

1. Remove groups with value 0.
2. Sort by value (highest first), then by ability to win, then by demand tier.
3. Quick wins: within each value level, move groups where an existing page already ranks in positions 5 to 20 to the front of that level.
4. Move a group down one place for reduced click opportunity, and two places for poor, staying within its value level.
5. Break any remaining ties by revenue: the group closest to the products that sell best goes first.

Seasonal deadlines aren't part of the score. The plan in SKILL.md step 8 handles them.

Write a one-line reason beside every group that makes it into the plan.
