# Content brief template

A brief should let a writer who has never met the business produce a page that fits the search and says something the ranking pages don't. If it contains only a keyword and a word count, it isn't finished.

```markdown
# Brief: [working title]

URL: [new slug or existing URL]
Group: [main query] + [other queries in the group]
Page type and format: [guide / comparison / category page / tool], [steps / table / list]

## Reader
Who is searching, what just happened that made them search, and what they will do after reading.

## The job of this page
One sentence: after reading, the reader can ________.

## What currently ranks
(If the results couldn't be checked, write "not checked" and list this as the writer's first task.)
- Top 3 results and what each does well
- What they all miss or get wrong
- Results-page features to plan for (questions box, video, AI answer)

## Our contribution
The thing this page has that the others don't: our own data, photos of the real process, a worked example from our customers, a calculator, an expert on staff. Name the source and who supplies it.

## Outline
H1:
H2s in the order the reader needs them, each with a line on what it covers.
Questions to answer directly (from People also ask, support tickets):

## Facts that must be right
Prices, specs, safety points, claims. Each with its source.
Who checks health, money, safety or legal claims before publishing:

## Links
In: existing pages that should link to this one, with the sentence where the link fits
Out: the product or category page this leads to; any authoritative sources to cite

## Call to action
The single next step, and where on the page it goes.

## Length
A range, set by what the reader needs. Note how long the top results are, but don't copy their length.

## Avoid
Claims we can't support, competitors' wording or structure, topics that belong on another page in the map.
```

## Filled-in sample (shortened)

```markdown
# Brief: How to get bubbles out of resin

URL: /guides/resin-bubbles (NEW)
Group: how to get bubbles out of resin + resin bubbles fix, heat gun resin, why does my resin have bubbles
Page type and format: guide, numbered steps with photos

## Reader
A hobbyist whose first or second pour came out cloudy with bubbles. They're deciding whether to sand it back or start again.

## The job of this page
After reading, the reader can stop bubbles forming on the next pour, and fix the piece they already have.

## What currently ranks
- A forum thread with mixed advice, some of it wrong (torching too close)
- A thin manufacturer post that is mostly a product pitch
- A video from a large craft channel
- Gap: nobody explains the room temperature range or shows what over-torching looks like

## Our contribution
Side-by-side photos from our workshop: the same pour at 16°C and 24°C room temperature. Our resin chemist's mixing-speed advice.
...
```
