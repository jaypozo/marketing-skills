# Page sections, in order

Use this as the default order. Drop any section the decision doesn't need. Move a section up if the evidence says it's the main worry; if price is the top objection, the price section can sit right after the hero.

## 1. Hero

- Headline: what this is and the outcome, for whom.
- Subhead: one or two sentences that make the headline concrete. Name the mechanism or the main detail.
- Primary button, and one line under it that removes the biggest remaining worry (no card needed, delivery time, free cancellation up to 48 hours).
- A picture of the thing doing the job: a screenshot of the interface, the product in use, the van and the team. Stock photos of smiling people tell the visitor nothing.
- For social ad traffic, reuse the ad's image or a close match, so the visitor knows they're in the right place.

## 2. The problem, briefly

Two to four lines in the customer's words about the situation that brought them here. Skip it for warm traffic who already know they have the problem. Give it more room for cold social traffic.

## 3. What they get and what changes

Three to five blocks. Each has a heading that states a benefit, a short body that explains how, and proof where you have it. Order them by what the evidence says buyers care about most, not by the feature list.

## 4. Proof

Put specific proof beside the claims in section 3 where you can. Then add a short block for the rest: review rating and count with the platform's name, a short case with a real number and a date, logos only with permission.

Look for testimonials that name a situation and a result, such as "We used to spend Sunday night on the schedule. Now it's done before lunch on Friday."

## 5. Objections

The reasons people hesitate, answered directly. Write them as a buyer would put them: "My staff won't download another app." Give the answer, then the proof. Practical questions go in the FAQ instead, so nothing is answered twice.

## 6. How it works, or what happens next

Three or four numbered steps from click to result. This matters most for services, bookings and anything delivered later.

## 7. Price and terms

State the price, what's included, any minimum term, and the refund, guarantee or cancellation terms in the words of the actual policy. If there are plans or sizes, say who each one is for in one line.

## 8. FAQ

Up to eight real questions taken from support, calls and sales. Don't invent questions to fill space. Practical questions only: delivery, compatibility, cancelling, data, setup time, access.

## 9. Final call to action

Restate the main outcome in one line and repeat the primary button with the same wording as the hero.

## Short-page version

Hero, then three blocks from section 3 with proof attached, then a price line and the final call to action.

## Extra sections for local service and booking pages

- Where you work: towns or areas served, in a line near the top, so out-of-area visitors don't waste a click.
- Availability, stated honestly: typical lead time, same-day options if real. Keep it current.
- The people: a real photo and first name of whoever turns up, with relevant certifications.
- What you need from the customer: access, parking, water or power, whether they need to be home.
- Before and after photos, with the customer's permission.
- Phone number as a tap-to-call link alongside the booking button.
- After booking: what the confirmation says, reminders, how to reschedule.
