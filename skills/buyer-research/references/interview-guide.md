# Customer interview guide

Hand this to the person running the interviews. Calls run 20 to 30 minutes. Record them with permission and send the transcript back for coding.

## Who to talk to

- 5 to 8 people who bought in the last 90 days. Memory of the decision fades fast after that.
- 2 or 3 people who showed clear interest and didn't buy: abandoned carts, expired trials, sales calls that went quiet.
- Not friends, staff or the most vocal fans. They tell you what you want to hear.

Offer a small thank-you, such as a gift card or account credit. More people agree when you do. Say up front that you aren't selling anything on the call.

## Ask about a past event

Ask about one specific past event, never about the future or about opinions in general. "Tell me about the day you ordered" gets specific answers. "Would you pay more for X?" gets polite guesses.

## Questions, in order

Opening (2 minutes)

- What do you do, and where does [product category] fit into it?

The trigger

- Take me back to when you first started looking for something like this. What was happening?
- What had you been doing about it before then?
- Why then and not six months earlier?

The search

- Where did you look first? Who did you ask?
- What else did you seriously consider? What did you like about it?
- Was there a point where you nearly gave up on the whole thing?

The decision

- What made you pick this one in the end?
- What did you need to see or hear before you were comfortable paying?
- What worried you right before you paid?

After

- What happened the first time you used it?
- Is it doing what you hoped? What's different from what you expected?
- If it disappeared tomorrow, what would you do instead?

For non-buyers, swap the decision and after sections for:

- What did you end up doing instead?
- What would have had to be different for you to buy?

## How to probe

- When someone gives a general answer ("it's easier"), ask for the last time it happened: "Can you walk me through the last time that was a problem?"
- When they use a vague word ("cleaner", "professional"), ask what it looks like: "What would you see that tells you it's professional?"
- Repeat their last few words as a question, then wait. People often add the useful part after a pause.
- Don't explain or defend the product. Write down the misunderstanding; it's a finding.
