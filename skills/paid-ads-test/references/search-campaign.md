# Test campaign setup

## Search campaign

One campaign per product or job type, each with two to four ad groups. Budgets are set per campaign, so a cheap group and an expensive group in one campaign will fight over the same money. Each ad group is one intent group: searches that mean the same thing and fit the same landing page.

| Setting | For a first test |
| --- | --- |
| Networks | Search only. Turn off the display network and search partners. |
| Location | Where the business can sell or deliver. Use "presence" (people in the area), not "interest". For service-area businesses, target the service radius or the counties served. |
| Language | The language of the ads and the page |
| Schedule | All hours to start, unless the business takes phone calls only at set times |
| Bidding | Manual CPC, capped at break-even. Switch to conversion bidding at about 30 conversions in 30 days. |
| Budget | Daily budget = campaign test budget ÷ test days. Google can spend up to about double on a single day while keeping the monthly total within the limit, so treat the cap as monthly. |
| Match types | Exact and phrase. No broad match in a first test. |
| Ads | One responsive search ad per ad group, with headlines that repeat the group's main words |
| Landing page | One page per ad group, matching the promise of the ad |
| Conversion | The real sale or lead, set as the primary conversion in the ad platform and tested before launch. If analytics conversions are also imported, keep only one of the two as primary so nothing is counted twice. |
| Calls (lead businesses) | Call assets on the ads, a call conversion for calls from the website, and a minimum call length (60 seconds is a common start) so wrong numbers don't count. Run call ads only during staffed hours. |

### Choosing keywords

1. Start from the buyer's words in the brief and the store's search queries.
2. Keep terms that show buying intent: product type plus a qualifier ("buy", "price", "for [use]", a specification, a location for local businesses).
3. Drop pure research terms ("what is", "how to") from a first test. They're cheaper but rarely buy.
4. Five to fifteen keywords per ad group is enough.

### Starting negative list

Adjust it to the business. Never add a negative that blocks a real buyer. A business that sells templates mustn't exclude "template". A repair business mustn't exclude "repair". Where insurance pays, "free" searches ("free windshield replacement insurance") may be real buyers.

- free, cheap (only if the business isn't the cheap option), download (for physical goods)
- jobs, job, career, salary, hiring, course, training (unless that's what's sold)
- how to, diy, tutorial, what is, meaning
- login, sign in, customer service, phone number, refund (existing-customer searches)
- used, second hand, rental, repair (when the business sells new)
- competitor brand names. If you test them, use their own ad group and budget, keep the brand name out of the ad text, and expect a lower conversion rate.
- places the business doesn't serve

### Review schedule

| When | Look at | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Ads approved, spend starting, tracking firing | Fix disapprovals and tracking |
| Day 3 | Search terms report | Add negatives, note good new terms |
| Day 7 | Search terms, CTR by ad group, CPC vs break-even | Pause keywords above break-even CPC with no conversions |
| Weekly | Cost per lead and per paying conversion vs break-even, by ad group. Impression share lost to budget and to rank. | Apply the stop rules |
| End | Total spend, conversions, CPA, revenue | Write the result and the next decision |

## Paid social (Meta, TikTok, Pinterest, LinkedIn)

Use paid social when people don't search for the product yet, or when it sells on sight.

- One campaign optimized for the real conversion, or for an earlier step when volume is too low (see the Meta rule in SKILL.md).
- One or two ad sets with broad targeting inside the right country and age range. Narrow interest stacks shrink delivery and rarely help small budgets.
- Three creative concepts, each built on a different message from the buyer brief, such as an objection answered, a result shown, or the product in use. Small budgets can't separate ten variations of one idea, so vary the message rather than the wording.
- The first second of a video, or the main image, has to show the product or the result. Captions on.
- Judge on cost per conversion and on what those customers do afterwards (refunds, repeat orders).
- LinkedIn clicks usually cost several times what other platforms charge. Use it only when the deal value is high and the buyers can be targeted by job title.
